# Research: 39 domain-industry sites, read directly

Read on 26 November 2026. Each page below was opened and read; the notes record what the page
actually showed that day. "Took" is what the finding changed in this site.

Not counted: three pages that could not be opened (Spaceship SellerHub's own page, a NameBio
daily report, one NamePros thread) and YouTube watch pages, which were rate-limited. Where a
figure for one of those appears on the site, it is labelled with where it really came from.

## What the reading showed

1. **The niche is crowded with marketplaces and news, and thin on neutral help for the seller.**
   Marketplaces explain their own product. News sites report deals and policy. Almost nobody
   explains, without a product to push, how to price a name, choose a venue and write to a buyer.
2. **Parking income collapsed in 2025 and 2026.** One analyst's account (Bill Hartzer) plus a
   registrar's own guide (IONOS) say the same thing: monetisation is no longer the default use
   of parking. That leaves selling, which is what "domain marketing" means.
3. **Fees are tiered and badly understood.** Sedo's rate runs from 10% to 20% depending on how
   the buyer arrived. Dynadot halves its rate if you bring the buyer. Escrow costs a fraction of
   a commission. Nobody shows these side by side.
4. **The best tools show their working.** Instant Domain Search reports three prices and every
   factor behind its score. GoDaddy returns a value, a list price and a probability of sale.
5. **Trade sites monetise with sponsors, not volume.** One reports its banner positions sold
   out. Sponsor banners, a newsletter and a podcast are the standard kit.
6. **Lead capture is weak across the board.** Most service pages offer only "Get started" or
   "Contact us". A pre-qualified request that arrives with the name, the goal and a draft plan
   attached is better for both sides.

## Deliberately not copied

- Banner walls above the content.
- Appraisals shown as a single confident number.
- Sign-up gates in front of calculators.
- Fake urgency: visitor counters, countdowns, "another buyer is interested".
- Any fee that the company did not publish where we could read it.
