The for-sale page: what belongs on it

6 minute read. Reviewed November 26, 2026.

The best traffic a domain for sale will ever get is someone typing the name itself to see whether it is taken. That person is already interested. The for-sale page has one job: let them act before they close the tab.

Five things the page must do

  1. Say it is for sale, in the first line. Not “coming soon”, not a logo. The words “this domain is for sale”.
  2. Show the name large. Seeing a name set as a headline is how a buyer pictures it on a sign or a slide.
  3. Give a price or a clear route to one. A buy-now figure, a make-offer button with a stated minimum, or “price on request” with a promise of a number.
  4. Offer one obvious next step. One button. Buy, offer or ask.
  5. Explain how payment is protected. Two sentences on escrow remove the biggest fear a first-time buyer has: paying a stranger for something invisible.

Exact price, range or nothing

An exact price converts best on names up to the low thousands, because a buyer can decide alone. A stated minimum offer filters out the $50 messages without capping the top. Showing nothing only makes sense for names where you truly want every inquiry, and you are willing to answer each one fast.

If you offer monthly payments, put the monthly figure beside the full price. Large portfolio sellers such as HugeDomains show a payment plan on their own for-sale pages, and investor Elliot Silver has pointed out that buyers who meet those pages first may come to expect the option everywhere. The payment plan tool gives you the figure and the wording.

Trust is technical too

  • HTTPS. A browser warning on a page asking for money ends the sale. Any modern static host gives you a certificate for free.
  • Speed. The page should be one small file. No frameworks, no tracking pile-up, no slideshow.
  • Works on a phone. A large share of type-in visits come from phones. Check yours on one.
  • A real route to a person. A form or address that reaches you, tested this month.

Chat widgets and other extras

Live chat helps if you will actually be there to answer within minutes. Some marketplaces build chat into their own pages for this reason. If you cannot staff it, an unanswered chat box is worse than none. The same goes for visitor counters, “3 people are looking at this name” banners and countdown timers: if they are not true, they are a lie, and if they are true, they rarely help.

What to write

One sentence on who the name suits is enough. “A short name for a payments product” does more than a paragraph about brand equity. Three plain selling points at most: length, how easily it is said aloud, the extension. Never claim traffic, revenue or interest you cannot prove on request.

Test it like a buyer

Open the name in a private window on your phone. Time how long it takes to understand that it is for sale and what to do next. Press the button and confirm the message arrives. Then ask someone outside the business to do the same while you watch. Fix whatever made them hesitate.

Do it now. Build a for-sale page.
