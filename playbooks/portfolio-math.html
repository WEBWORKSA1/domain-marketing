Portfolio math: sell-through, renewals and the drop list

5 minute read. Reviewed November 26, 2026.

A domain portfolio is a small business with one kind of cost and one kind of income. Four numbers describe it completely, and most owners know only one of them.

The four numbers

  • Names held. The count you renew each year.
  • Carrying cost. Names held multiplied by your average renewal fee. This is due whether or not anything sells.
  • Sell-through rate. The share of your names that sell in a year. One sale from a hundred names is 1%.
  • Average net price. What you actually keep per sale after commission.

Profit is sales income minus carrying cost minus whatever you spent on new names. Everything else is detail.

A worked example

Take 400 names at $12 a year to renew. Carrying cost is $4,800. Suppose 1.5% sell, so six names a year, at an average of $2,400 before a 15% commission. That is $14,400 gross and $12,240 kept, for a profit of $7,440 before any new purchases.

Now change one input. If sell-through is 0.5% instead, two names sell, you keep $4,080, and the portfolio loses $720 a year. Nothing else moved. This is why sell-through, not the size of the list, is the number to watch.

The floor price for a name

Each name has to earn its own renewal. Divide the renewal fee by your sell-through rate and by the share you keep after commission. In the example above: $12 ÷ 1.5% ÷ 85% ≈ $940. A name that could not realistically sell for about $940 is, on average, costing you money to hold. At 0.5% sell-through the same sum gives about $2,820.

Bill Hartzer’s 2026 analysis of holding costs reached a similar order of magnitude for a hand-registered .com, which is a useful sanity check on your own figure.

Three levers, in order of speed

  1. Cut. Dropping names below the floor improves the result at the next renewal date. It is the only lever that works immediately.
  2. Raise sell-through. Prices on every name, working for-sale pages, a network listing, and outreach on the best ten. These take a few months to show.
  3. Raise average price. Better buying, and firmer negotiation on the top tenth of the list. This takes years, because it depends on what you acquire.

A yearly routine

  1. Once a year, export the list and record the four numbers. Use real figures from the past twelve months, not hopes.
  2. Rank names by your honest price estimate. Mark everything under the floor.
  3. For each marked name ask one question: is there a specific reason it is worth more than it looks? Traffic, an inquiry on file, a buyer you can name. If not, let it go.
  4. Before a marked name expires, offer it to other investors at wholesale. Small money is better than none.
  5. Put the renewal money you saved into marketing the top of the list, not into more names.

Being honest with the inputs

The calculator is only as good as what you type into it. Two habits keep it honest. Measure sell-through over at least two years, because one good sale distorts a single year. And count every cost: renewals, marketplace subscriptions, tools, and the names you bought and later dropped.
