Parking is over. Selling is the business now.

6 minute read. Reviewed November 26, 2026.

For twenty years the default answer to “what do I do with an unused domain?” was to park it and collect ad clicks. That answer no longer works for most names, and the consequence is simple: a domain you are not using earns money one way, by being sold.

What changed

Search consultant Bill Hartzer pulled the numbers together in an August 2026 analysis. In his account, Google stepped back from advertising on parked domains in stages through 2025, one long-running parking company announced its shutdown in January 2026, and the listed companies that run the remaining parking platforms reported steep falls in that line of revenue. His conclusion for a typical portfolio is blunt: list the names for sale or let them go.

You do not have to take one analyst’s word for the direction. IONOS, a large registrar, now tells its own customers that monetisation is no longer the default use of parking and should not be treated as a dependable core strategy. Parking products still exist. GoDaddy sells CashParking plans, for example, with an 80% revenue share on its paid tier. But a share of a shrinking number is still a shrinking number.

The cost side moved the other way

While ad income fell, the cost of holding a name rose. Hartzer notes that the wholesale price registrars pay for a .com goes to $10.97 from November 1, 2026, with a small ICANN fee on top. Your registrar adds its own margin. Every name in the portfolio now has to justify roughly a dozen dollars a year with something other than clicks.

The drop test. Take a name’s realistic sale price, multiply by the share of your names that sell in a year, then by what you keep after commission. If the answer is below the renewal fee, the name is expected to cost more than it returns. The portfolio calculator does this for the whole list.

What replaces parking income

Nothing replaces it passively. What replaces it is marketing, which is to say doing the things that make a sale more likely:

  • A price. A visible buy-now price, or a clear offer route, on every name you would sell.
  • A page. The name itself is a traffic source: people type it to see what is there. A for-sale page turns that visit into an inquiry. A blank page or an error wastes it.
  • Distribution. A listing on at least one marketplace network, so the name shows up when someone searches a registrar for something similar.
  • Outreach for the handful of names where you can name the buyer.
  • A smaller list. Dropping the names that fail the test above is a marketing decision too. It frees renewal money and attention for the names that can sell.

A plan for this quarter

  1. Export your list with renewal dates. Sort by the next 90 days.
  2. Score each name and write down a realistic marketplace price. Be honest about the bottom third.
  3. Run the drop test. Let the failures expire, or sell them to other investors at wholesale for whatever they fetch.
  4. Put a price and a for-sale page on everything that survives.
  5. Pick the ten names with an obvious buyer and send thirty good emails in total.

None of this is glamorous. It is the difference between a portfolio that costs money every November and one that pays for itself.

Do it now. Run your portfolio numbers.
