Where to sell a domain, and what each route costs

6 minute read. Reviewed November 26, 2026.

The cheapest venue is rarely the right question. A 5% route that nobody visits earns less than a 20% route that puts the name in front of a buyer mid-search. Choose on reach first, then on fee.

The four routes

1. Marketplace networks

Networks syndicate your priced listing into the search results of many registrars. Someone looking for a name at their registrar sees yours as a premium option and can buy it in the same checkout. This is the nearest thing to passive selling that still works, and it is why most investors list their priced names on at least one network.

The price of that reach is the top of the fee scale. Sedo publishes its structure clearly: 10% when a priced name is parked with Sedo or uses its sales page and sells directly, 15% for make-offer and auction sales, and 20% whenever the buyer arrives through the partner network.

2. Registrar marketplaces

Several registrars run their own marketplaces for names held in your account. Listing takes a minute and fees sit around 10%: Namecheap charges a flat 10%, and Dynadot charges 10% on listings and auctions, dropping to 5% when you bring the buyer yourself through a payment link. Reach is narrower than a network, so these suit lower-priced names and quick sales.

3. Brokers

A broker goes out and finds the buyer. DomainInvesting.com’s guide puts typical commissions at 10% to 20% and notes that nearly every broker will ask for an exclusivity period. That is a fair price when the name is worth six figures and the buyer has to be found and persuaded. It is poor value on a $2,000 name, and most brokers will decline it anyway.

4. Your own page plus escrow

Point the name at a for-sale page you control and close through an escrow service. There is no commission, only the escrow fee: Escrow.com starts at 2.6% with a $50 minimum on deals up to $5,000, and the percentage falls as the price rises. You keep the buyer’s details and the whole negotiation. You also do all the marketing.

Matching the name to the route

If the name is…Start withWhy
A solid two-word .com priced under $5,000A marketplace network at a fixed priceVolume and impulse purchases; the network does the finding
A coined, brandable nameA curated brandable marketplaceBuyers there are shopping for a brand, with logos and payment plans on show
A one-word .com or a short acronymA broker, or make-offer on your own pageThe buyer’s budget sets the price; a public number caps it
A name with an old site, links or trafficA marketplace that sells websites as well as namesBuyers there value the history
A name you want gone this monthAn investor community or a registrar auctionWholesale price, same-week sale

Three rules that save money

  • One price everywhere. If a buyer finds the name cheaper on a second venue, you have negotiated against yourself.
  • Know which fee applies before the sale. On tiered venues the rate depends on how the buyer arrived, not on what you hoped.
  • Read the exclusivity clause. Some venues and nearly all brokers restrict where else you may sell. Check before you sign, not after an offer arrives elsewhere.
