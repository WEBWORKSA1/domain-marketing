Work out a lease-to-own or payment plan

Choose a structure, a term and a premium over the cash price. You get the monthly figure, the schedule and a sentence to paste into your listing.

The three structures

  • Lease-to-own splits the total into equal monthly payments. It is the simplest plan to explain and suits most names.
  • A deposit plan takes a larger first payment and spreads the rest. Sellers use it on higher-priced names to see commitment up front.
  • A balloon plan keeps the monthly figure low and ends with one large payment. It suits buyers who expect funding or revenue before the term ends.

Setting the premium

You are waiting for your money and carrying the risk that payments stop, so charging somewhat more than the cash price is reasonable. Show both figures so that paying in full is visibly cheaper. Keep the term short enough that renewals and platform fees do not consume the premium. More in the payment plans playbook.

Questions people ask

Who holds the domain during the plan?

Not the buyer. Either a platform, an escrow holding service or you, with the name’s settings pointed where the buyer asks. Ownership passes after the final payment.

What happens if the buyer stops paying?

Under the usual terms the buyer loses use of the name and the payments already made. Put the grace period and the consequence in writing before the first payment.

Is this a loan?

A lease-to-own plan is normally structured as a lease with an option to buy, not as credit. How it is treated legally and for tax depends on where you and the buyer are, so check before you offer plans at scale.
