Does your portfolio pay for itself?

Four inputs describe a portfolio. Change any of them and see the yearly result, the sell-through you need to break even, and the price a name has to fetch to earn its renewal.

Use your real figures from the last twelve months. The defaults are an example, not a benchmark.

The sums

  • Carrying cost = names held × average renewal.
  • Expected sales = names held × sell-through rate.
  • Income kept = expected sales × average price × (1 − commission).
  • Profit = income kept − carrying cost − new purchases.
  • Floor per name = renewal ÷ sell-through ÷ (1 − commission). A name that cannot realistically sell for this much is expected to cost more to hold than it returns.

Using the floor

Sort your list by an honest price estimate and mark everything below the floor. Those names are candidates to drop or to sell wholesale before renewal. The portfolio math playbook walks through the yearly routine.

Questions people ask

What sell-through rate should I use?

Your own, measured over at least two years: names sold divided by names held. One good year or one large sale will mislead you if you use a single year.

Does this include tax?

No. Tax treatment of domain sales differs by country and by whether you trade as a business. Ask an accountant who knows your situation.

My result is negative. Now what?

Cutting names below the floor is the only change that helps at the very next renewal. After that, work on sell-through: prices and for-sale pages on every name, and outreach on the best few.
