Glossary

45 terms a domain seller meets, in plain words.

Aftermarket
The market for domain names that are already registered, as opposed to registering an unused name for the standard fee.
Appraisal
An estimate of what a name would sell for. Automated appraisals read the name and sales data; human appraisals also weigh buyers, timing and use.
Authorisation code
A password for a single domain, issued by the current registrar, that the buyer needs to move the name to a different registrar. Also called an auth code or EPP code.
Backorder
An instruction to a drop-catching service to try to register a name the moment it is released. You usually pay only if the attempt succeeds.
Balloon plan
A payment plan with lower monthly payments and one large final payment.
BIN
Buy it now. A fixed price at which a name can be bought without negotiation.
Brandable
An invented or loosely suggestive name valued for how it sounds and looks, not for a dictionary meaning.
Broker
A person or firm paid a commission to find a buyer for a name, or to acquire a name for a client.
ccTLD
Country-code top-level domain: a two-letter extension assigned to a country or territory, such as .ca or .de. Each sets its own rules.
Comparable sale
A past sale of a name similar in length, type, extension and industry, used to estimate a price. Often shortened to comp.
Concierge escrow
An escrow option where the escrow company takes the name into its own registrar account during the handover, then passes it to the buyer.
Drop
The moment the registry releases an expired name so that it can be registered again.
Drop-catching
Sending automated registration requests at the instant a name drops, on behalf of customers who placed backorders.
End user
A buyer who intends to use the name for a business or project, as opposed to an investor buying to resell.
Escrow
A neutral service that holds the buyer's payment until the name has been transferred, then releases the money to the seller.
Exact match
A name made of the exact words people use to search for a product or service.
Expired auction
A sale of unrenewed names run by a registrar or its partner during the grace period, before the name would otherwise be deleted.
Extension
The part of a domain after the last dot, such as .com. Also called a top-level domain or TLD.
Fast transfer
A marketplace feature where the seller pre-authorises the registrar to move a name automatically when it is bought, so the buyer gets it within minutes.
Floor price
The lowest amount a seller will accept. Also, on this site, the price a name must be able to fetch to justify its renewal.
For-sale page
The page shown when someone visits a domain that is for sale. Also called a lander or landing page.
Grace period
The time after expiry during which the registrar still lets the owner renew at the normal price. Set by each registrar, up to 45 days.
gTLD
Generic top-level domain: extensions not tied to a country, such as .com, .net and .org, and the newer ones such as .app.
Hand registration
Registering a name that nobody currently holds, at the standard fee.
Inbound
Interest that arrives without you asking for it: a visitor to the for-sale page or a marketplace inquiry.
Lease-to-own
A plan where the buyer pays monthly, uses the name from the first payment and owns it after the last. Often shortened to LTO.
Liquid name
A name that can be sold to another investor quickly at a predictable price, such as short letter or number combinations on .com.
Make offer
A listing with no fixed price, inviting the buyer to propose one.
Marketplace network
A marketplace that also shows its listings inside the search results of partner registrars.
Minimum offer
The lowest offer a for-sale page or listing will accept, shown to filter out unserious bids.
Nameservers
The servers that tell the internet where a domain's website and email live. Changing them points the name at a different host or for-sale page.
Outbound
Contacting potential buyers directly instead of waiting for them to find the name.
Parking
Pointing an unused domain at a page of advertising links to earn money from visitors. Once a common source of income; much less so since 2025.
Pending delete
The final five days before a name is released. Nothing can be done to it during this stage.
Premium domain
Loosely, any name priced above the standard registration fee, either by a registry or by its current owner.
Push
Moving a name between two accounts at the same registrar. Faster than a transfer between registrars.
Redemption period
The 30 days after the grace period in which only the previous owner can recover a name, for an added fee.
Registrar
A company accredited to sell and manage domain registrations for customers.
Registry
The organisation that operates an extension and keeps the authoritative record of every name under it.
Reserve
The confidential minimum price below which a name will not be sold at auction or by a broker.
Sell-through rate
The share of a portfolio that sells in a year. One sale from a hundred names is a sell-through rate of 1%.
Type-in traffic
Visits from people who type a domain directly into the address bar instead of searching for it.
UDRP
The Uniform Domain-Name Dispute-Resolution Policy, the process trademark owners use to challenge a registration they say was made and used in bad faith.
Wholesale price
What investors pay each other for a name, usually a small fraction of what an end user would pay. Also, the fee a registry charges registrars per name.
WHOIS / RDAP
The public lookup systems that show who registered a domain, where, and when it expires. Much personal detail is now hidden by default.
